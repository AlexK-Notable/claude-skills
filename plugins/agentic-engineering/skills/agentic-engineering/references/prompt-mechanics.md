## T3.1 — Serial position and frontloading: what's actually known `[MEDIUM]`

The honest version of "put important things first":

- Primacy and recency biases are real and measured, but the U-shaped lost-in-the-middle effect is **conditional on window occupancy** — strong up to ~50% occupancy, dissolving into recency-dominance beyond it (`context-degradation.md` T2.3; open-source models only).
- Position is **one of three established degradation mechanisms** — lexical matching and length itself are the others, ranking among them open (T2.2). Neither placement nor phrasing makes a long window free.

Defensible practice: frontload instructions and identity-critical content *when operating at low occupancy* (the usual case for curated agent prompts); anchor must-survive instructions near the end as well when windows run hot; never rely on position to rescue content that lacks lexical overlap with how it will be queried.

## T3.2 — Instruction-following mechanics `[MEDIUM — adversarial batch 2026-06-13]`

Only the constraint-interference result (MOSAIC) carries `[HIGH]`; the rest of this section is `[MEDIUM]` or single-probe.

- **Dropping an instruction is an enforcement failure at generation time, not a memory failure.** The knows-but-violates rate — recites the constraint on probe (~97% recall), violates it while generating — ranges 8% to 99% across models, and **does not track model tier in any obvious way** (GPT-5.4 8%, Gemini Pro 18%, Qwen3-235B 55%, Gemini Flash 76%, Llama-3.3-70B 93%, a Sonnet variant 99%) — our reading, not a result: on seven models the paper runs no correlation test and files the relationship as open `[MEDIUM — arXiv:2604.28031, single preprint, multi-turn ideation domain]`. Consequence: **reminding a high-violation model of a constraint it already knows does not fix the drop** — re-injection answers an enforcement problem with a memory fix.
- **Constraints cost you the task, not your obedience.** Add self-evident constraints — ones the model's own unconstrained answer already satisfied — and constraint satisfaction stays flat near the ceiling (averaging over 94%) while *task* success falls (SustainScore averaging 84%). The damage is front-loaded: most of it lands within the first ~5 constraints, after which the curve flattens `[MEDIUM — arXiv:2601.22047]` (eight models, 1 to 16 keyword constraints, one math task; the plateau is a measurement argument for 5 constraints, not a ceiling on how many you may write). Practical rule: spend your first three-to-five constraints on what matters most, and grade the deliverable rather than the checklist — ticking every box while getting the answer wrong is the documented failure mode here.
- **The failure *shape* is model-tier-dependent — but its thresholds move with each model generation.** As instruction count climbs (10–500 tested), stronger models hold near-perfect then drop sharply at a threshold, mid-tier models degrade linearly, weaker models exponentially `[MEDIUM — IFScale, arXiv:2507.11538; cite the curve taxonomy, not the absolute scores]`. The ~150-instruction cliff is a 2025 artifact: a May 2026 replication found frontier models pinned at 100% at N=500 and had to widen the keyword vocabulary from 500 to 10,000 words before any degradation reappeared, pushing the breaking point from roughly 200–300 simultaneous constraints to roughly 2,000 `[CORROBORATED* — Arize replication, 12 May 2026]`. Keep the taxonomy; treat every published threshold as generation-specific and re-measure on the model you ship.
- **Constraints interfere with each other.** Opposed instructions trade off measurably — negative inter-constraint correlations of −0.28 to −0.34 between readability and forced keyword inclusion, and −0.32 to −0.44 between "avoid X" / "use X" style twins. The sharpest conflict measured is not a semantic twin at all: −0.531 between a token-count requirement and respond-in-JSON, a length budget fighting a structural format `[HIGH — MOSAIC, arXiv:2601.18554, EACL 2026, marketing-copy domain]`. Constraints don't compose for free — conflicting ones silently degrade each other, and format constraints count as constraints.
- **Positive exemplars beat prohibitions, with a mechanism.** Naming a forbidden token tends to *prime* it: ~87.5% of "do not say X" violations were priming failures where mentioning X activated it `[MEDIUM — single model, Qwen-2.5-7B, arXiv:2601.08070; frontier transfer untested]`. Corroborates the vendor guidance to show desired output rather than enumerate prohibitions, and adds the **ironic-rebound** caveat: restating a prohibition can make it worse.
- **In multi-turn work, reinforce in the *first* follow-up turn.** 74% of multi-turn instruction drift first violates by turn 2 of a 6-turn task; periodic reflection checkpoints reduced violations for some models (Qwen 55%→36%) but not others (Llama 93%→92%) `[MEDIUM — arXiv:2604.28031]`.
- **Turning on thinking redistributes instruction-following errors rather than removing them.** Across Qwen3 and Hunyuan on IFEval, the aggregate pass-rate change from built-in thinking is small (−0.55 to −3.52pp), but 10–20% of prompts flip between pass and fail: planning-type constraints (global counting, structure, coordination) improve, while precision constraints on exact local form consistently worsen `[CORROBORATED* — arXiv:2606.09662, single-author preprint, post-stamp]`. Extended thinking is therefore not a blanket fix for a dropped format rule: a formatting violation is a reason to move the constraint, not to raise the thinking budget.

**Placement (system vs user turn) — thin evidence, not a void.** The circulating "+32/+37.4pp on Gemini" figures have a source: arXiv:2603.25056 §4.2, a 2×2 factorial varying security framing independently in system prompt and user turn. System framing moved the metric far more than user-turn framing (+32.0pp vs +0.9pp on Gemini 3 Flash; +37.4pp vs +10.6pp on Gemini 2.5 Flash). Not a general law of placement: the metric is phishing-detection recall for an email-triage agent, and the interaction term is strongly **negative** (−27.4pp and −30.7pp) — framing in *both* slots scored below the main effects, so the second slot cost recall rather than adding to it `[MEDIUM — arXiv:2603.25056, preprint, security-detection domain]`. Directional reading: if a framing gets one good home, the system prompt is usually it, and echoing it into the user turn is not free. What *is* settled `[VENDOR-DOC]`: recent frontier models follow instructions more literally, so over-aggressive "CRITICAL: YOU MUST" language written for older models now overtriggers — dial it back to plain statements of when an action applies.

## T3.3 / P1.5 — Structured output `[CORROBORATED* — Opus probe 2026-06-13]`

Settled mechanics `[VENDOR-DOC]`: platform structured outputs guarantee schema-valid JSON via `output_config.format` / strict tool schemas; schema compilation is cached (~24h); constraints compose with tool use; refusals and token-limit truncation escape the guarantee. The empirical "why/when" beyond the mechanics — labelled per bullet below, with `[HIGH]` carried only by the latency bound:

- **The 2024 "structured output hurts reasoning" alarm did not replicate on a same-prompt basis.** The origin result ("Let Me Speak Freely?", EMNLP 2024) tested no frontier reasoning models and was attributed to methodology — different prompts per condition and a faulty AI parser; a same-prompt rerun found constrained generation *matched or beat* free-form on every matched pair (Llama-3-8B Last Letter: 0.77 vs 0.73 under the JSON prompt, 0.68 vs 0.65 under the natural-language prompt) `[CORROBORATED*, vendor-interested on both sides]`. The honest delta is ~3–4 points, in constrained's favour; the ~12-point gap people quote pairs the best structured run against the worst free-form one — the cross-condition error the rebuttal exists to name. Do not cite the original alarm's magnitudes as current.
- **The real, reproducible residual cost is field ordering.** Generation follows schema order, so a schema that puts the *answer field before a reasoning field* forces the model to commit before it thinks `[CORROBORATED* — mechanism matches autoregressive generation]`. **Fix (free, now consensus): declare a free-text `reasoning` field first — and mark it required.** On Anthropic, required properties are emitted before optional ones regardless of declaration order, so an *optional* `reasoning` field placed first silently lands after the answer and buys nothing; the documented remedies are to make every property required or to absorb the reordering downstream `[VENDOR-DOC]`. Nothing errors, which is why it survives review. Reasoning models largely sidestep this by emitting thinking tokens before the JSON block: grammar state resets between sections, so only the final output is schema-bound `[VENDOR-DOC]`.
- **Constraining can *raise* accuracy, not merely reliability** — JSONSchemaBench measured ~1–4 point gains over free generation across GSM8K / Last Letter / Shuffle Objects, a safety-net effect. Scope: one model (Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, itself fine-tuned for schema adherence), and the paper makes no claim the benefit is largest on small models `[MEDIUM — arXiv:2501.10868, single model tested]`.
- **Latency is rarely the deciding factor.** Optimized engines add tens of microseconds per token (XGrammar, arXiv:2411.15100; sometimes net *faster*, since constraints shorten output); the only real cost is one-time grammar compilation on complex *uncached* schemas, which vendor docs bound with an explicit compile timeout `[HIGH]`.
- **Constrained decoding cannot signal that the input is out of schema.** Handed something the schema doesn't describe, it fills required fields with schema-valid garbage (the false-confidence failure: a banana receipt → `quantity: 1` instead of an error). The one refusal the grammar can't suppress is the platform's own — a safety refusal returns `stop_reason: "refusal"` as a normal 200 and escapes the schema entirely — but it is a safety escape hatch, not a semantic one, and will not fire because your input was wrong. Pair strict schemas with an explicit `error`/refusal field or a free-text escape hatch `[CORROBORATED*, vendor]`. Relatedly, over-typing a field (e.g. `integer` where the true value is 51.7) forces a wrong answer — leave latitude where the model needs it.

**Decision rule:** constrain when the consumer is a machine and the model can't be trusted to emit valid JSON (weaker models, closed-enum extraction) — reliability gain, often an accuracy gain. Prefer free-generation-then-parse when the task needs hard reasoning you can't front-load, or when the model must be able to refuse invalid input. The `reasoning`-field-first pattern collapses most of the tradeoff for reasoning-bearing structured tasks.

## P1.1 — Dispatch-prompt anatomy `[ANECDOTAL]`

Every worker dispatch is a contract with four mandatory parts:

1. **Objective** — one sentence, first line. What this worker is *for*, not how to proceed.
2. **Output format** — what the orchestrator will parse, stated concretely: field names, section headings, "a list of absolute file paths", "each finding as `file:line` plus one sentence". A vague format ask returns a vague shape.
3. **Scope boundaries** — what is IN and what is OUT, both written down. `tool-design.md` P3.2 carries the externally-supported version: workers fail along MAST category 2 (misalignment) when boundaries are implicit, because silence transmits nothing.
4. **Context the worker cannot discover itself** — decisions already made, constraints already ruled on, file paths, prior findings, dead ends already walked. Everything else it can read for itself, and reading fresh costs less than a stale summary misleads.

The ordering is the prompt-level detail P3.2 doesn't carry: **objective first, boundaries before the context dump.** A worker that meets the context block first spends its opening attention reconstructing why it was called. State the deliverable's consumer explicitly — "your final message is parsed by a program, not read by a human" — because a worker that believes it is writing for a person writes narration, and narration does not parse (P3.2 item 3).

## P1.2 — Purpose-first exploration `[ANECDOTAL]`

In exploration and research dispatches, state *why* the information is needed before *what* to find.

A worker given only a shopping list returns exactly the list — including the list's blind spots. A worker that knows the purpose makes correct relevance judgments at every fork the prompt failed to anticipate: it follows the adjacent file that actually answers the question, it stops when the purpose is served rather than when the list is exhausted, and it reports the finding that invalidates the premise instead of filing it as out of scope. The cost of omitting purpose is silent — nothing in the return says "I found something you didn't ask for and dropped it."

Related mechanism: the knowledge-action gap (`caching-and-knowledge-delivery.md` T6.2) — a step the agent must *choose* to take gets skipped under pressure. Stating the purpose converts "decide whether this is relevant" from a judgment call into a comparison against a criterion the worker already holds.

## P1.3 — Compaction-resilient anchors `[ANECDOTAL]`

Long-running work gets summarized, and a summarizer preserves what looks like a rule while paraphrasing what looks like an aside. Three practices for instructions that must survive that pass:

- **Stand the instruction on its own line, declaratively.** A constraint embedded mid-paragraph inside its own rationale is prose, and prose gets rewritten. The same constraint as a standalone imperative line reads as a rule and comes through. This is the prompt-side counterpart of `caching-and-knowledge-delivery.md` T6.4's preserve-verbatim list (architectural decisions, unresolved bugs, exact constraints, and identifiers) — write the constraint in the shape the summarizer is already told to keep.
- **Repeat load-bearing constraints near the end as well as the top.** T3.1's positioning rule is occupancy-conditional: frontloading is the low-occupancy case, and a prompt long enough to be summarized is exactly the case where the tail also matters. Repetition is not free — see the negative interaction term under Placement above, in a different domain — so spend it on the two or three constraints whose loss would invalidate the work, not on the whole instruction set.
- **Keep identifiers verbatim, every time.** Paths, branch names, IDs, exact flag spellings. T2.2 supplies the mechanism: retrieval degrades when the query's surface forms stop colliding with the stored text, and paraphrase-heavy summaries are precisely what breaks the collision. An identifier that survives one round as a description ("the config file") cannot be searched for in the next.
