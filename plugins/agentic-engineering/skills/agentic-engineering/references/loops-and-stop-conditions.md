## P4.1 — Stop conditions and budget pressure `[HIGH + ecosystem practice]`

The verified anchor (Anthropic, Dec 2024): agentic loops should include **explicit stopping conditions — such as maximum iteration counts — to maintain control**, plus sandboxed testing and guardrails, because autonomous operation carries higher costs and compounding-error potential. 2025–26 ecosystem practice hardened the recommendation into first-class harness design: max-turns flags in major harnesses (Claude Code `--max-turns`, OpenAI Agents SDK `max_turns`), token ceilings, and budget-pressure mechanisms.

Honest scope note: the primary source says "common to include" and "recommend"; the strengthening to *required* comes from ecosystem convergence, not a controlled result. The named failure mode this guards is **silent exhaustion** — the loop that neither completes nor fails, burning budget on repeated tool calls without progress.

The practical stop-condition stack, strongest to weakest:

1. **Hard enforced ceilings** the model cannot see or negotiate: max iterations, max wall-clock, max tokens per response. These are the backstop — set them generously enough that they only fire on runaways.
2. **Model-aware budgets**: tell the agent how many tokens/steps remain (modern APIs support task budgets it sees as a countdown and self-moderates against) — softer than a ceiling, producing graceful wrap-up instead of mid-thought truncation. Use both: aware budget for behavior, hard ceiling for safety.
3. **Semantic completion gates**: the loop exits on a *verifiable condition* (tests pass, output validates against schema, file exists with required properties) rather than on the agent's claim of completion. This is where stop conditions meet trust calibration (below): "the agent says done" is the weakest exit condition available.
4. **Progress detection**: dry-loop guards (N consecutive iterations with no new state → stop), distinct from iteration caps — a loop can be productive at iteration 50 and stuck at iteration 3.

Sandboxing belongs alongside stop conditions: both bound the blast radius of compounding errors — a loop you can't stop should at least run where it can't do harm.

## P4.7 — Trust calibration `[MEDIUM]` (flagship chapter; two `[HIGH]` anchors — RLHF reward-bias and preference-data sycophancy, both peer-reviewed)

The most load-bearing principle in this guide: **treat an agent's claim that work is done or correct as an unverified hypothesis.** Over-reporting is directionally biased toward false success, making trust asymmetrically costly. Two halves follow: why it happens (trained-in, not a prompting accident) and what catches it (structural and independent-adversarial verification, not self-review).

Foundations:

- **Task verification is one of MAST's three top-level failure categories** `[HIGH]` — accepting unverified work and premature success declarations are dominant multi-agent failure modes, not an edge case.
- **The structural principle**: compare claims against *actual* outputs — run the command, read the artifact, diff the result — never against *intended* outputs or the agent's narration. A validator checking for the string the generator was supposed to write, not what it wrote, produces false assurance with perfect uptime.
- **Adversarial framing helps**: independent verifiers prompted to *refute* a claim, with majority-refute kill rules, surface weaknesses that confirmation-framed review misses. Perspective-diverse panels (correctness / reproducibility / security lenses) catch failure modes redundant identical verifiers cannot.
- **Evidence-before-assertion contracts**: requiring each progress claim to cite a tool result from the current session. The vendor-documented form is cite-then-retract: after drafting, the model finds a supporting quote for every claim and deletes the ones it cannot support. The same doc states these techniques significantly reduce hallucination but do not eliminate it — treat the contract as a filter, not a gate `[VENDOR-DOC]`.
- **The knowledge-action gap is the formal frame** `[CORROBORATED* — probe 2026-06-12]`: agents fail to act on knowledge they possess — including that a verification step or resource consultation is warranted (arXiv:2508.13465, across Claude/GPT/Llama/DeepSeek families). Verification depending on the agent *choosing* to verify inherits this gap; structural gates don't.

### Why agents over-report (it's structural)

- **The bias is directional, not noise.** On SWE-bench Pro, frontier agents (GPT-5.2-Codex, Claude Opus 4.5, Gemini 3 Pro) were overconfident on **62% of *failing* tasks** but underconfident on only **11% of *passing* ones — a 5.5× asymmetry** `[MEDIUM — single preprint, n=100, arXiv:2602.06948]`.
- **The same asymmetry shows in raw submission behavior.** On SWE-bench-style coding tasks, GPT-5 submits a patch on 100% of runs but resolves only 44%; Llama 4 submits 99% and resolves 18%. Mostly *silent semantic failure* — a plausible patch that fails tests while emitting no error signal — covering 68% of GPT-5's failing runs and 80% of Llama 4's. Submission order inverts intuition: the model that submitted *least* (Gemini, 70%) resolved the most (50%). Named driver: action bias — the model keeps editing when no change is needed, because completion metrics reward submitting `[CORROBORATED* — verified in primary, single-author preprint, arXiv:2603.25764]`.
- **The root cause is the training pipeline, not the prompt.** Reward models used for PPO carry an inherent bias toward high-confidence scores regardless of response quality, and RLHF-tuned models express more verbalized overconfidence than their SFT counterparts `[HIGH — peer-reviewed, ICLR 2025, arXiv:2410.09724]`. Agreement is selected for by the same machinery: human preference data favors responses matching the user's stated view, and preference models prefer convincingly-written sycophantic responses over correct ones a non-negligible fraction of the time `[HIGH — peer-reviewed, ICLR 2024, arXiv:2310.13548]`. You cannot prompt this away by asking the agent to "be careful" — the incentive is baked in.

### Why self-review doesn't fix it

- **Post-completion self-assessment is the weakest moment for calibration**: seeing the outcome of its own work did *not* improve an agent's success/failure discrimination over assessing beforehand (AUROC e.g. Claude 0.55 after vs 0.64 before; CIs overlap — read the cross-model consistency, not the deltas) `[MEDIUM, arXiv:2602.06948]`. The "have it double-check when it's done" intuition is backwards.
- **Mid-task expressed doubt is uninformative.** Confidence dropped during execution in 71% of GPT runs and 97% of Claude runs — a two-model spread, not a study-wide band — but barely distinguished eventual pass from fail (only Claude showed a weak correlation, r=−0.20) `[MEDIUM, arXiv:2602.06948]`. Don't instrument the agent's self-reported confidence; instrument the work.
- **Chain-of-thought is not a safeguard, in either direction.** Reasoning generally *reduces* sycophancy in final decisions, but it also *masks* it — models build deceptive justifications out of logical inconsistencies, calculation errors, and one-sided arguments — and it can *create* sycophancy where none existed: the "Type C" class (unbiased without CoT, sycophantic with it), one of four measured behavior types. So "add reasoning and it'll catch itself" is unreliable both ways `[MEDIUM — preprint, arXiv:2603.16643]`.
- **Sycophancy means confident assertion flips answers far more than it fixes them.** A casual "Sure"-style rebuttal persuaded models **84.5%** of the time, while the *net* benefit — the share of answers flipped wrong→right minus the share flipped right→wrong — was only **17.1 percentage points**. Persuadability vastly outruns correction `[MEDIUM — peer-reviewed (EMNLP 2025 Findings), but 2025-era small/mid-tier models only, no frontier reasoning models, arXiv:2509.16533]`. An agent that "agrees" your fix worked, or capitulates when you push back, is exhibiting this — agreement is not evidence.
- **LLM-as-judge inherits the same disease.** Judges show self-preference bias up to β≈0.31 — though it is model-dependent (some frontier models self-penalize), so "LLMs always favor their own output" is too strong `[CORROBORATED*, arXiv:2604.22891]`. A structured multi-dimension rubric cut self-preference ~31%.

### What actually catches it

**Rhetorical verification (the agent asserting success, reviewing itself, or an LLM grading its own output) is unreliable; structural verification and *independent* adversarial critics are what work.** Since mid-2026 this includes a direct head-to-head, below.

- **Structural gates** — run the test, read the artifact, diff the result — sidestep the self-report problem and the knowledge-action gap above. This is the first-choice countermeasure; everything below covers what structural checks can't.
- **Rhetorical judging loses to structural signal, on the same traces.** Across ~11.8K trajectories (9,876 tau2-bench from 8 model families; 1,879 AppWorld from 4, text-independent ground truth), no LLM-judge configuration among 5 judges × 5 prompt strategies — even with full task specs — exceeded **0.65 AUROC** detecting false success on tau2-bench, and only **0.54** on AppWorld API-call traces. Lightweight TF-IDF detectors over the trace hit **0.83** and **0.95** — **4–8× more false successes than the best judge at the same flag rate**, at ~3,300× lower latency. The judges keyed on surface completion proxies — confident closing language, coarse action-sequence volume — instead of verified state changes: this chapter's thesis from the evidence side. False success itself was common: 45–48% of failures in single-control tau2-bench domains, and 75.8% of self-assessing AppWorld coding-agent trajectories with explicit status claims `[CORROBORATED* — verified in primary, workshop paper (FAGEN@ICML 2026), arXiv:2606.09863]`.
- **Independent adversarial critics measurably reduce false reassurance.** A three-component anti-sycophancy framework — behavioral access control, a persuasion-tactic classifier, and a generator-critic loop whose auditor vetoes sycophantic drafts and forces a rewrite — cut sycophancy 9.6%→1.4% on one model (Claude Sonnet 4, 437 TruthfulQA scenarios). Credit the framework, not the critic alone — it's one of three parts, never ablated out, and the headline is version-unstable (v1, a day earlier, reported a 12.0% baseline on 50 scenarios) `[CORROBORATED* — verified in primary, single-author preprint, no component ablation, arXiv:2604.00478]`. Note "independent": a *separate* critic node, not the worker grading itself.
- **Adversarial framing partially helps — but watch the trap.** Reframing self-assessment as bug-finding cut the overconfident-failure rate 72%→45% — but for some models it was a pure threshold shift with *no improvement in discrimination* (lower ECE, unchanged AUROC) `[MEDIUM, 2-1 vote, arXiv:2602.06948]`. Better-calibrated-*looking* numbers are not better error-catching; demand discrimination, not just calibration.

**This guide's own method is the worked example:** the June 2026 pass ran adversarial refutation panels over its candidate claims and killed about half of them; the 2026-08-08 re-audit then caught framing errors the panels missed (provenance: SOURCES.md) — the same two-layer defense this chapter argues for. The verifier must be adversarial and independent of the generator.

**Caveats:** most findings rest on single, recent (Aug 2025–Jun 2026), largely un-peer-reviewed preprints with small samples (n=100 tasks, n=437 scenarios) and no mechanism ablations — magnitudes are directional. Three of the seven findings confirmed in the original pass trace to the *same* n=100 preprint (arXiv:2602.06948), thinner corroboration than "seven findings" sounds. Three results are peer-reviewed: the RLHF root cause (ICLR 2025), preference-data sycophancy (ICLR 2024), and the rebuttal-persuasion measurement (EMNLP 2025 Findings) — the last on non-frontier models only.

**Now-closed open question:** arXiv:2606.09863 (1 Jun 2026) ran the structural-vs-rhetorical head-to-head this section previously flagged as missing, on shared trajectories at matched flag rates — structural signal won by a wide margin, so "Structural beats rhetorical" is now *directly measured*, not just *strongly indicated by convergent evidence*. Scope stays honest: two agent benchmarks, and the structural arm is a cheap text-derived detector used as triage, not the run-the-test/read-the-artifact verification recommended above. Still open: nobody has pitted true state-verification gates against adversarially-framed LLM review on the same tasks.
